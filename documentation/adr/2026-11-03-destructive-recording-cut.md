# Cutting a recording deletes the cut part; there is no hide mode

Owners can cut the beginning and end of a recording. The cut deletes those parts from stored audio and every transcript. The remaining timeline starts at zero, and the processing pipeline requests fresh derived results. There is no saved hidden range and no cut annotation in the recording view.

**Superseded in part** by [shared recordings cut in place](2026-11-08-shared-recordings-cut-in-place.md): cuts preserve Organization placements, Organization facts receive the same keep-as-manual exception as private facts, generated title markers in either stored view count, and intended sharing waits for an active cut. The destructive operation and all other rules still hold.

**Superseded in part** by [sentence-boundary recording cuts](2026-11-08-recording-cut-sentence-boundaries.md): sentence starts replace turn midpoints for retention, turns can be split, typed times snap to sentence starts, transcript controls sit at paragraph boundaries, and scissors appear only in the transcript header. The remaining destructive-cut rules still hold.

## Why

People mainly cut private talk before sharing. A hidden range must be respected by every reader: Organization colleagues, downloads, backups, folder exports, search, MCP and API clients. A reader that misses the range reveals exactly what the owner wanted removed. Deleting the data gives those readers one recording to handle.

## Options considered

### Delete the cut parts (chosen)

- **Pros:** readers and exports consume the same retained content; there is one mode to explain.
- **Cons:** no undo; audio processing and regeneration cost time and may incur provider charges.

### Keep the full recording with a visible range (declined)

- **Pros:** instant adjustment, reversible edits and no audio rewrite.
- **Cons:** the private content remains stored and available to readers that ignore the range.
- **Rejected because:** honoring the range becomes a privacy requirement on every existing and future reader. One omission defeats the purpose of cutting. Reconsidering this would require a separate product purpose and a complete access contract for retained originals.

## Decision

Delete the cut parts. Only the owner can request the operation. A shared recording follows the existing withdrawal confirmation and retention warning before the destructive change. Organization facts follow normal withdrawal. Confirmed facts in the owner's private Almanac stay as manual assertions if the cut removes their last supported evidence. Only a title generated by Chatterbox is cleared for regeneration; other titles stay.

## Key technical details

- `POST /api/recordings/[id]/cut` queues `recording.cut` through the existing durable queue, with at most one active cut per user across worker processes. The selected transcript revisions and original audio identity fence the transaction.
- Audio preparation streams through temporary files and a new storage key. Compressed stream copies can retain samples beyond an edge, so compressed formats are re-encoded before publication. Duration and waveform are verified before the database change.
- The recording lock protects the audio pointer, encrypted transcript rewrites and derived-data cleanup. Encrypted preparation and cleanup checkpoints make crash recovery retryable. Cleanup holds the recording and claim locks and preserves every referenced audio key.
- Turn midpoints decide retention; retained times are clamped and shifted. The shared renderer rebuilds text, and the rewrite hook preserves exact speaker labels and accepted correction anchors on retained turns.
- Summary rows, task proposals, content vectors, Learn review payloads and AI agenda suggestions in every scope and state are removed. Accepted tasks and agenda points, private confirmed facts and owner remarks stay. Source locks fence in-flight agenda suggestions. The private title step identifies generated titles; only those are cleared to the existing empty-title value. Regeneration follows the profile's transcript source and provider settings, including transcription of cut audio when required.
- A private confirmed fact losing its last supported recording evidence becomes a manual assertion so it remains visible. Organization facts use ordinary withdrawal pruning. Accepted tasks retain their human decisions; proof quotes and times survive only when the retained turns still support them. Transcript revision checks prevent an in-flight embedding write from restoring stale vectors.
- Nullable migration `0153` adds `recordings.cut_at`. Plaud sync and in-flight Plaud imports check it, and Restore audio refuses it. Storage filename reconciliation holds the recording lock and defers while a cut is active.
- Browser audio responses carry the audio hash and cut timestamp. A browser save begun before a cut cannot restore old transcript text afterward; browser transcription of the retained audio remains available.
- Folder export planning already fingerprints audio identity and rendered document bytes. The cut schedules the existing planner; no separate export implementation is added. Backups include the retained content and cut timestamp. The hosted v1 response shape stays unchanged.

## Consequences & open follow-ups

- There is no undo. The original remains in Plaud and in older exports and backups. A retention-reaped recording can still have its transcripts cut.
- Existing folder-export behavior applies, including keeping previously exported reaped audio and orphaned artifacts when their source disappears. Cutting cannot recall copies already obtained elsewhere.
- Both self-host and hosted receive the same additive migration, without new environment variables or compose changes. A failed preparation leaves the original intact. A committed cut affects its owner and authorized readers; recovery of removed content requires an external original or older backup. Application rollback cannot restore it and must not run an image that ignores the Plaud cut marker.
- A real-account Plaud sync and a visual browser review remain deployment checks; tests use a simulated Plaud client and component rendering.
