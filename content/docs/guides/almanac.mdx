---
title: The Almanac
description: The people and things Riffado knows, how to edit them, how Learn adds to them, and importing a list.
---

The **Almanac** is what Riffado knows about the people and things your
recordings talk about. It names speakers, corrects misheard names in
transcripts, and keeps facts ("Jana works on Orion"). Open it from the top
navigation. It has four tabs:

- **People**: everyone who speaks or is spoken of.
- **Things**: organizations, teams, projects, products, terms, locations and
  documents.
- **Vocabulary**: the kinds of things and relations your knowledge uses,
  beyond the built-in ones.
- **Review**: Learn's proposals waiting for you (self-hosted instances).

## People and things

Add a person on the People tab, and a thing on the Things tab: pick its
type, give it a name, and optionally nicknames and a description. On a
record's page you can:

- **Edit** its name (and a thing's description). Transcripts that link to
  it read the new name.
- **Change type** of a thing, say a product that is really a project. If a
  fact of yours about it would no longer fit (nobody *leads* a term), the
  change is refused, naming that fact's relation: change or erase it
  first.
- **Merge into…** another record of its type, when two records are one.
  Nicknames, facts and links move to the one you keep.
- **Erase** it, with its nicknames and facts. For a thing, the corrections
  linking to it go too, and transcripts show those words as they were
  heard.

## Nicknames

**Also known as** lists a record's other names: the nicknames people gave,
and how transcription heard the name (from corrections you accepted). Add a
nickname there, and remove the ones you added. Learn uses nicknames to
recognize a name however it is said, and searching People or Things finds
a record by them too.

Names are also recognized in their word forms, in the transcript's
language: "Šimákem" finds Šimák, "Forestu" finds Forest, with or
without accents. Czech, Slovak, Polish and Romanian are covered for
missing accents too; English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Dutch,
the Nordic languages, Finnish, Hungarian, Russian, Slovene, Turkish and
others by their stems.

## Facts

**What is known** lists the facts about a record, each with the recordings
that support it. **Add a fact** picks a relation that fits the record (a
person *works for* an organization, a term *means* some text), then the
other side: someone or something from the Almanac, or text.

A relation that holds one value at a time (*works for*) asks before a new
value replaces the current one. Facts you type are kept until you change or
erase them; facts from recordings stay while a recording supports them.

Riffado does not keep knowledge about people's health, family,
personality, performance or demographics. Text typed about a person that
names such a topic is refused, by a fixed word list.

## Learn

**Learn** on a recording's transcript proposes names for unnamed speakers,
corrections of misheard names, and facts, from what the Almanac already
knows. Nothing changes until you review the proposals. After a run, the
Learn button says how it went and opens what the review did with each
proposal, with **Re-learn**.

Learn also proposes **new people and things** it hears: a client, a
project, a product, a term the team uses, or someone mentioned by their
full name. They are listed first in the review, under **New in the
Almanac**, unticked. Tick one to add it, correcting its name or its type
first if needed, or say which record it already is (**It is someone
known…**). Proposals that refer to a new record (a speaker who introduces
themselves, a fact about a new client) wait until that record is ticked.

A proposal close to something the Almanac has asks **Maybe it is …,
misheard?**: a name a letter off, a first name only one known person has,
or a known name inside a longer one. When you say which record a proposal
is, what Learn heard becomes that record's nickname ("Honza" for Jan), so
the next run recognizes it. A name the record's own name already says
("Milan" for Milan Petrák) is not kept: it would make every Milan him.

A new record you mark **Never propose it** is not proposed again on any
recording; one you only leave unticked is not proposed again on that
recording. Re-learn with "also propose again what I rejected" brings back
the ones rejected on that recording.

## Names from the Almanac

Transcription with ElevenLabs Scribe v2 or Speechmatics is told which names
to expect, so it writes them right the first time. Up to 500 names go with
each recording, from your records and the Organization's (on the
Organization view, the Organization's only):

1. you (the person with your account email);
2. the people and things your transcripts named in the last 90 days, the
   most often named first, then those named earlier;
3. records transcription misheard before;
4. the rest.

Each record gives its name and its nicknames. Speechmatics also gets how
the name was misheard in the recording's language ("Tavesy" for Tavesi).
A name longer than 49 characters or 5 words is left out.

These names go to the transcription provider with the audio, including
names of people who are not in that recording. ElevenLabs charges this as
an add-on to its hourly price.

## Import a list

On the Things tab, **Import a list** takes one line per type: the type, a
colon, and names separated by commas. Nicknames go in parentheses.

```text
product: Alpha, Beta (Béta, B2)
organization: Acme
person: Jana Nováková (Janička)
```

The type is a type's name or key, in English or Czech, singular or plural.
A preview shows what each name would do: be added, exist already (its new
nicknames are added to it), or be skipped because its type is unknown or
several people have that name (give those nicknames on the person's page). An
import never creates types: add them in the vocabulary first. Up to 500
names at once.

## Your records and the Organization's

On an instance with an Organization, the Almanac shows your own records and
the Organization's. Only the organization account changes the
Organization's; you can keep private notes on them, add your own nicknames,
and fold a private duplicate into one. See
[Organization](../self-hosting/organization#people-and-things).
