# Restricted Organization folders withhold placements until normal publication succeeds

An Organization folder can have a restricted audience. Its placements do not publish content to the Organization: items retain their owners, processing, knowledge and receiver rights until the normal share gate releases a placement. Server APIs and additive schema changes support a separate UI change. This record covers the server implementation of issue #381; the UI and the independent person-shared-folder leaf change are separate parts of that issue.

**Superseded in part.** [Removing contributors from restricted folders](2026-11-11-removing-contributors-from-restricted-folders.md) reverses rule 3's contributor-removal refusal. Managers and curators may remove item owners without unfiling their items. It also narrows rule 1's placement privacy: an owner outside the audience sees their own placement through the public node and may remove or move it out without folder access. The remaining rules still hold.

## Why

Teams need an audience inside their Organization tree before they disclose content to everyone. Existing Organization filing is a publication operation, not just an access check: it promotes people, vocabulary and knowledge, rebinds profiles and tasks, changes retention, and permits Organization exports. Hiding the tree alone cannot undo those effects. Curator authority must come from current `org.curate` verification, while `access.manage` remains content-blind.

### Previous state

An item was shared whenever it had any Organization-owned placement. Every member could see that tree. Private folder participants already had receiver access without publication, but their folders belonged to individual users.

## Options considered

### Antechamber placements (chosen)

Store restriction on the Organization folder and withholding on each placement. Reuse private receiver views, publication, withdrawal and durable jobs. This preserves two knowledge scopes, but requires auditing all ownership-based publication predicates and metadata discovery paths.

### Filter ordinary Organization sharing (declined)

The existing tree, jobs and knowledge model could remain unchanged with fewer schema additions.

**Rejected because:** Organization filing already publishes knowledge and changes ownership-independent processing and retention. Viewer filtering cannot preserve an owner's private knowledge or recall earlier Almanac additions.

### A third knowledge scope (declined)

A folder-specific knowledge base would offer a distinct collaborative workspace.

**Rejected because:** it requires separate vocabulary, identity resolution, processing, backup and lifecycle semantics. Restricted folders need receiver access to owner content, not another independently maintained knowledge base.

## Decision

**Chosen: antechamber placements.** The maintainer rulings in issue #381 define the contract:

1. Restricted folder nodes stay visible to every human Organization member. Hiding them was rejected on 2026-11-10 because members created look-alike folders and duplicated the tree. Public metadata is the folder ID, name, path, tree position, restriction/access flags, managers' names and only the viewer's own request state. Contents retain owner semantics and publish no Organization knowledge. Items, counts (including ancestor totals), placement chips, content events, tasks, agendas, suggestions, mail addresses/ingest, folder knowledge, defaults, export configuration, other audience identities/roles and restrict/lift previews remain audience-only. Folder-level refusals use 403 `RESTRICTED_FOLDER_NO_ACCESS`; unreadable items keep 404. Service callers see no restricted nodes.
2. Audience members and current curators read; `access.manage` grants no content access. In local authorization the Organization account is the curator.
3. Anyone who may create an Organization subfolder may create a restricted one. The creator manages its audience. Any audience member or curator may add people or decide an access request; only managers and curators may remove people. Contributors cannot be removed while they have placements there. An active human member outside the audience may request access; there is at most one pending request per person/folder. All audience members receive the existing invitation email and in-app invitation; curator authority alone sends no notification. Approval adds a member atomically; decline notifies the requester and permits another request. Direct addition approves a pending request. Selected recipients are revalidated as active Organization members with `use` under the tree lock; recipients who lost eligibility while waiting are skipped and reported. Cancellation, lift, folder deletion and loss of Organization membership close pending requests.
4. Restricted folders are leaves. They may be moved under ordinary restructuring rules, but cannot contain folders.
5. Curators may restrict an open leaf. Owners join its audience. Items losing their last open placement follow the existing withdrawal path. Earlier Almanac promotions remain.
6. Only owners write restricted content. Other readers use receiver views without queuing processing.
7. Restricted tasks follow receiver visibility and assignee completion rights. Item-reader metadata supports assignment warnings.
8. Another open placement shares the item normally through the gate and publication path.
9. Managers or curators lift without another approval. Passing items release immediately; failing items keep their placements withheld, preserve the former audience and gain a confirmed pending share for later execution. Cancelling that pending share withdraws its intended filing: remove its backing withheld placement in the same tree-locked transaction, then drop the former audience when no withheld placement remains. Stale executors skip the deleted intent.
10. Owner retention governs withholding and the first 30 days after release. Organization retention applies from the 30-day boundary.
11. MCP service accounts and Organization subtree exports exclude withheld content explicitly. Curator access is not an export entitlement. Clarification: the Organization archive is also the owner archive of the Organization account, so it carries every item that account owns, including restricted and unfiled content. Members' withheld-only items remain in their personal owner archives and are excluded from the Organization archive.
12. Restricted folder mail accepts only audience senders through the normal ingest policy.
13. Colour and icon belong to the separate UI change.
14. There is no automatic lift date.
15. Making person-shared private folders leaves belongs to the separate sibling branch; this implementation adds only an independent restricted-container guard.

## Key technical details

- `recording_folders.restricted_at` stores completed restriction. `restricted_folder_members` stores manager/member roles and membership provenance. Suspension or loss of Organization access retains these rows, including contributors, so returning members keep their place. Every membership read and authority check applies live recipient eligibility (not suspended and, in IdP mode, active with `use`); stale rows confer no access and do not appear in current audience lists. Assignments gain `withheld` and `released_at`.
- The additional folder field closes a transition hazard. `restricting_at` identifies an in-progress durable restriction: each item is atomically withheld and withdrawn, then the last transaction sets `restricted_at`. Until then the folder remains open and unprocessed placements retain their prior open semantics. The Organization account archive follows this open-until-processed rule; live exports stop earlier because external copies cannot be recalled. New filings are withheld and subfolders are refused. Ordinary sibling-name uniqueness applies to every folder; the unreleased `name_conflict_exempt` column and archive field are removed because public nodes no longer need an exemption. Restricting and lifting never change name-conflict behavior.
- Each operation durably queues every item in insert batches of 250 before processing at most 25 in the request. The handler has concurrency 2 and five attempts; database claims permit one running restriction job per owner. Retries check the transition epoch and placement state; deleted folders and superseded transitions do not recreate data.
- Arrival acquires the Organization tree lock before insertion only for an Organization-owned target folder or an explicit agenda attachment touching the Organization tree. Folder ownership is stable. Deferred recorder metadata is resolved before creation; unfiled, private, personal-mailbox and local-only arrivals do not acquire that lock. Automatic matching never files into the Organization. Deferred recorder finalization and video extraction fall back to an unfiled recording when the locked recheck finds the folder missing or returns `RESTRICTED_FOLDER_NO_ACCESS`; the folder and its selected profile are cleared. Ordinary uploads still refuse.
- Identity-provider authorization occurs before row locks. Item transactions follow Organization tree advisory lock, Organization people lock, task graph locks, item lock, and the existing processing/profile locks in the publication or withdrawal path. Membership and stored holder/curator authority are rechecked on the transaction executor under the tree lock, including at restrict/lift authorization before state changes or job creation. Restriction requires a current curator; lifting requires a current curator or an active manager with `use`. Nested arrival, attachment, profile and filing queries use the transaction executor; locked checks never reserve another pool connection or verify against the identity provider.
- `restrictedItemCondition` is viewer-aware; publication predicates remain viewer-independent and require an open, nonwithheld placement. The predicate guard requires a reviewed reason for every folder-owner comparison outside the canonical helpers.
- Public tree-node projection is separate from private folder-detail and placement checks. Structural tree invalidations carry an internal structural marker and reach every human member, with their internal folder scope and marker stripped before delivery; recipients reload public node metadata. Scoped placement invalidations, item events, placements, pending intents, addresses, knowledge aggregation and meeting sources retain audience checks. Meeting folder-filter predicates authorize the matching placement as well as the item: human callers use current placement visibility, and service callers require an open, nonwithheld Organization placement. Human MCP `list_folders` exposes restriction/access flags; recorder discovery retains only fileable folders for released extensions. Reader-warning metadata also hides audiences of placements invisible to that caller. Open agendas exclude restricted-only sources, including carry-over provenance. Existing accepted text remains.
- Release grace follows additional open placements, moves and deleted-folder fallback into the Organization root. An already open preexisting placement keeps its previous retention authority.
- Restrict and lift previews expose counts, owners, gate failures, retention grace and ancestor exports. Share provenance returns recorded person/entity promotion identifiers and declares `provenanceComplete: false`; it cannot reconstruct all older promotions.
- External materialization rechecks publication immediately before calling its provider. A write already in the provider may finish after restriction; the next exclusive export plan removes the revoked placement's tracked files, including Drive files. Export eligibility stops when restriction is authorized, including placements still waiting for their atomic withdrawal; cleanup cannot be followed by regeneration while the folder is `restricting`. The transaction authorizing restriction stamps all existing copies before queuing item work; transactions withholding a placement or moving an open placement into a restricted destination also stamp `folder_export_materializations.revocation_pending`; moving, unfiling or deleting the source cannot cancel that obligation. Materialization source IDs are historical references without cascading item/folder foreign keys; writer/configuration foreign keys remain. Failed removal retains the marker and owned node until the owned file is removed or confirmed absent, regardless of its content version. An unresolved owned revocation fails the plan with retryable `EXPORT_REVOCATION_PENDING`, so the worker backs off and retries without another mutation. Deletion cleanup uses the existing export retry classifier: transient failures remain pending; permanent failures return non-retryable `EXPORT_REVOCATION_BLOCKED` with the connection, target or provider cause. Both retain the pending obligation. Configuration and configured-root/ancestor deletion acquire exclusive export locks, remove only pending revoked copies outside database transactions, then recheck under the tree lock before cascading. Newly marked obligations or newly configured subtree exports refuse deletion with retryable HTTP 409 `EXPORT_REVOCATION_PENDING`. Ordinary export removal preserves its existing copy semantics. Foreign files remain protected, including inside managed directories. Drive directories are retained: `drive.file` gives per-file access and a child listing cannot prove the absence of foreign descendants, even several levels inside export-created directories. Filesystem pruning still removes only empty owned directories. Existing configurations on a newly restricted root may reconcile for cleanup only; they cannot materialize content or authorize a new configuration. Ordinary Drive orphan preservation still holds.
- Archive metadata is `organization/restrictions.json`, referenced by the manifest. Organization archives contain tree state and audience identities, published member content, and all Organization-owned content. Personal archives retain the owner's Organization placement references and paths, including tombstoned original meeting items retained by a live owned recording. Restore maps these through the returned meeting-item ID map and current audience checks before detach. Genuinely deleted items remain excluded. Organization-owned mail uses the common archive predicate. Every Organization placement carries withholding and release timestamps, including already released placements. Restore adapters resolve Organization audiences by email on empty restored folders; personal withheld references refile into existing restricted folders only with current audience membership. A gate-failing lift is archived as an open folder with a still-withheld owner placement. Restoring that reference creates a confirmed pending share, with no placement and no access for the former audience; the normal owner share gate creates the placement and publishes it when it passes. Open-folder metadata never restores the former audience. Existing released references preserve their release timestamp through the normal share path only when the resulting placement is open; a restored withheld row keeps `releasedAt` null.
- `restricted_folder_access_requests` stores requester, pending/approved/declined/cancelled state, decision attribution/times and decision-notice dismissal. A partial unique index enforces one pending request per person/folder; a pending-folder index supports the invitation inbox. Decisions recheck audience/curator authority under the Organization tree lock and insert membership in the same transaction. Recording loss of Organization membership takes that lock before updating access state and cancelling requests, serializing it with creation and approval without making provider calls under the lock. Pending requests and decision notices are transient and are not archived.
- Migrations are generated with Drizzle; schema additions are inert for existing open folders and both deployment modes. Existing installations need their normal release migration process, with no environment or Compose changes.

## Consequences & open follow-ups

- A restriction over more than 25 items completes asynchronously. The API returns `restricting: true` while old open placements are withdrawn transactionally; callers must refresh folder state. Permanent job failures require retry before the folder can finish restricting.
- Every sibling name remains unique, including restricted nodes. The ordinary conflict helps members reuse the existing tree and request access.
- Earlier person/entity promotions are intentionally not recalled. The preview exposes available provenance, not a complete historical reconstruction.
- The separate UI must consume reader metadata for assignment warnings, indicate queued transitions and pending releases, and implement the open-placement confirmation.
- The Organization account's archive includes its restricted and unfiled items by ownership. This is a clarification of ruling 11, separate from service-account and subtree-export publication eligibility. Member-owned withheld-only items remain excluded.
- Placement profile defaults deliberately ignore a restricted folder's bound Organization default, including for owners and verified audience members. Owner defaults apply while withheld; using an authorized restricted-folder default remains a limitation.
- No general archive importer is introduced. These bounded restore adapters preserve the new metadata and owner placement references alongside existing import adapters.
