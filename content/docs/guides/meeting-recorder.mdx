---
title: Meeting Recorder
description: Record meeting audio in your browser and send it to Chatterbox for transcription and summaries.
---

The Chatterbox Meeting Recorder is a Chromium extension that records a meeting's
audio and uploads it to your Chatterbox instance, where it is transcribed and
summarized like any other recording.

It captures your **microphone** and meeting audio from a browser tab. Native
Zoom or Teams desktop audio is available only when Chrome offers system audio
for screen sharing on your computer. Audio is mixed by default. The optional
split mode places your voice on the left and everyone else on the right.

Chromium browsers only: Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi. Firefox and Safari do not
let extensions capture system audio.

## Install

Open **Settings → Meeting Recorder** and click **Install from Chrome Web Store**.
The extension works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and Vivaldi.

For extension development only, open `chrome://extensions`, enable **Developer
mode**, choose **Load unpacked**, and select `extensions/recorder` from the
repository.

## Connect it to Chatterbox

1. After installing, open your Chatterbox dashboard and click the extension's
   toolbar icon. Choose **Connect to** followed by your server address.
2. Read the three-point data-use notice and click **I understand and agree**.
   The full notice and privacy policy expand below it. There is no checkbox.
3. Click **Allow microphone**. Choose Chrome's first, persistent option:
   **Allow while visiting the site** (or **Allow on every visit**). **Allow this
   time** expires. Edge and Brave show neutral guidance for permanent access.
   You can choose **Not now**; the saved skip warns that recordings will lack
   your voice and lets you allow the microphone later.
4. Click **Connect to** the displayed server and allow site access. This is a
   separate click from microphone permission. Chatterbox sends the recorder key
   automatically. The extension shows **The extension is ready** and offers
   **Back to Chatterbox**. Other settings appear after setup.

If you opened Options directly, it can find open recorder settings or onboarding
tabs. Choose the host and tab title if several are found. If none is found,
enter your Chatterbox address; the extension opens recorder settings in a new
tab. Sign in there if needed. Setup remains active for 15 minutes.

On an older server, after about five seconds the extension offers to focus the
Chatterbox tab. Open **Settings → Meeting Recorder** if necessary and click
**Connect**. A key from the tab and server chosen during setup is accepted
without another confirmation. Proposals from elsewhere still need confirmation.
With an older extension, keep using **Connect** on the Chatterbox page and confirm
the server in the extension as prompted.

For manual pairing, expand **Connect manually** in Chatterbox and click
**Generate recorder key**. Open the extension's **Options**, read and agree to
the data-use disclosure, complete or skip the microphone step, expand **Enter
address and key manually**, and paste the address into **Server URL** and the key
into **Recorder key**. Click **Save and grant access** and allow this server in
Chrome. Open tabs of that server detect the extension without a reload.

Revoke a recorder key any time from the same page or under **API Keys**.

## Automatic recording

Google Meet, Microsoft Teams (web) and Zoom (web) default to **Prompt**. This
also applies after updating an installation with no saved platform choice.
Other platforms stay **Off** unless you or the server choose otherwise. Saved
choices take precedence over server defaults, except when the server locks a
platform to **Auto**. Server defaults take precedence over the initial modes.

With **Prompt**, when a detected meeting becomes audible, a notification with
the participant reminder offers **Consent confirmed · Record** and **Not now**.
To keep only the **REC?** badge, turn off **Show a notification when a meeting
is detected** in **Options**. Chrome does not let a notification capture a tab,
so **Consent confirmed · Record** opens Chrome's sharing dialog: choose the
meeting tab and keep **Share tab audio** on. A window or the screen carries no
meeting audio, and the recorder window says so when the **Others** meter stays
silent. If the microphone is not allowed yet, a small window asks for it first,
or offers **Record without my microphone**. Clicking the toolbar icon instead
records the meeting tab directly, without the dialog. **Not now** dismisses the
prompt for that tab.

For one-click recording, open **Options → Automatic recording**, set a platform
to **Auto**, confirm the recording policy once and click **Save settings**.
From then on, when you open a meeting on that platform:

1. The toolbar icon shows **REC?** (and, if enabled, a notification).
2. With microphone access already allowed, one click on the icon or the keyboard
   shortcut starts recording directly, without the popup or picker. If microphone
   permission is missing, recording waits for your choice as described below.
3. Recording stops by itself when you leave the meeting page, close the tab,
   or the meeting goes quiet for a few minutes (3 by default).

That single click is required by Chrome: an extension may only start
capturing after you invoke it. Nothing can start a recording on its own, and
not clicking is how you skip a meeting you do not want recorded.

A self-hosted server can set defaults for everyone with
`RECORDER_AUTO_RECORD_PLATFORMS` (for example `google-meet,zoom,teams` or
`all`), plus `RECORDER_AUTO_STOP_QUIET_SECONDS`, `RECORDER_NOTICE_TEXT` and
`RECORDER_LOCK_DEFAULTS`. Users can override the defaults in the extension;
locking prevents changing server-selected **Auto** platforms. Supported
platforms: Google Meet, Zoom (web), Microsoft Teams (web),
Webex, Slack huddles, Whereby, Jitsi Meet. Native desktop apps have no tab to
detect and keep the manual screen-picker flow.

If the keyboard shortcut does nothing, Chrome may have left it unassigned;
the Options page shows the current binding and links to Chrome's shortcut
settings.

## Record a meeting

1. Click the extension icon, read the short participant reminder and choose
   **Consent confirmed · Record**.
2. If asked, allow the microphone or choose **Record without my microphone**.
   If Chrome opens its audio picker, choose the meeting tab and turn on
   **Share tab audio**, or choose a screen as described below.
3. Talk. Stop from the extension popup, or with the keyboard shortcut
   (`Ctrl+Shift+U` by default).

How meeting audio is captured depends on how you start:

- **Meeting in a browser tab** (Google Meet, or Zoom/Teams web): the extension
  can record that tab's audio directly when started from the toolbar with
  microphone access already allowed. Starts from the notification or microphone
  permission prompt use Chrome's picker instead. Choose the meeting tab and turn on
  **Share tab audio**. Other apps' sounds are not captured.
- **Another browser tab**: select the tab in Chrome's picker and turn on
  **Share tab audio**.
- **Native Zoom or Teams desktop app**: there is no tab to capture, so the
  extension opens Chrome's picker. Choose **Entire screen** and turn on
  **Share system audio** if available. If Chrome says screen audio cannot be
  shared, use the meeting's browser version instead. On macOS, Chrome may ask
  for **Screen Recording** permission the first time. A small recorder window
  stays open with microphone and meeting-audio levels. You can minimize it;
  closing it stops capture and uploads the audio saved so far. The same window
  appears when you select another browser tab in Chrome's picker.

When you stop, the extension finishes uploading the audio, then the server
assembles and saves the recording. The extension opens your dashboard with the
new recording selected after the server confirms it is saved. You can download
a playable copy from that recording's page.

## Microphone

The recorder uses your system default input unless you choose another device
under **Options → Microphone → Input device (override)**. Choose
**Default (recommended)** to follow the system default, or select a microphone
from the list, then click **Save settings**. The extension cannot read which
microphone your meeting app chose.

Missing microphone permission pauses a start until you allow access or choose
**Record without my microphone**. The popup warns beforehand and offers
**Grant microphone access**. The toolbar can show **MIC!** beforehand; on detected
meetings in **Prompt** or **Auto**, it shows **REC?** instead.

If Chrome blocks access, the extension shows this path:
`chrome://settings/content/microphone` → **Not allowed to use your microphone**
→ **Chatterbox Meeting Recorder**. Reset the microphone permission there, return
to the extension and allow access. For a managed browser, ask your administrator.

If the selected microphone is missing or unplugged when recording starts,
the extension retries the system default input. If that also fails, meeting
audio keeps recording. A persistent notification and a warning in the popup
explain the failure. If a microphone disconnects during recording, meeting audio
also continues with these warnings.

After explicitly starting without your microphone, you can choose
**Dismiss microphone permission reminders** in the popup. To restore them,
enable **Show microphone permission reminders in the popup and toolbar** in
**Options** and click **Save settings**. Microphone failure warnings during a
recording remain visible.

## Reliability

Audio is uploaded in short segments as it is captured and buffered in the
browser's private storage, so a brief network drop or a closed tab loses at
most a few seconds. If the browser closes mid-meeting, the extension offers to
finish uploading what it captured the next time it starts.

## A note on consent

Recording other people can require their consent, and the rules differ by
country and by meeting platform. The notice you confirm before each recording
is a reminder and an accountability record, not consent in itself. You are
responsible for informing participants and complying with the laws and platform
terms that apply to you.
