---
title: Meeting Recorder
description: Record meeting audio in your browser and send it to Riffado for transcription and summaries.
---

The Riffado Meeting Recorder is a Chromium extension that records a meeting's
audio and uploads it to your Riffado instance, where it is transcribed and
summarized like any other recording.

It captures your **microphone** and the **system audio** your computer plays,
so it works both for meetings in a browser tab and for native Zoom or Teams
desktop apps. The two are recorded on separate channels — your voice on the
left, everyone else on the right — which helps Riffado attribute who said what.

Chromium browsers only: Chrome, Edge, Brave, Vivaldi. Firefox and Safari do not
let extensions capture system audio.

## Install

The extension is not built into Riffado; it is a separate, load-unpackable
folder under `extensions/recorder` in the repository, or a packaged zip.

1. Open `chrome://extensions` and turn on **Developer mode**.
2. **Load unpacked** and select the `extensions/recorder` folder (or unzip a
   release and select that folder).
3. Open the extension's **Options** and grant microphone access.

## Connect it to Riffado

Open **Settings → Meeting Recorder** in Riffado. The page detects the
extension in your browser and tells you what to do:

- **Set up recorder**: one click. Riffado creates a write-only key (it can
  create recordings but cannot read your library) and hands it to the
  extension. Nothing to copy.
- **On a self-hosted Riffado, the first time only**: Chrome requires you to
  allow the extension to reach your server once. Click the extension icon,
  choose **Connect to your-server**, and the settings page updates by itself.
  Then click Set up recorder.
- **Manual setup** is always available at the bottom of the page if you prefer
  to paste the key into the extension's Options yourself.

Revoke a recorder key any time from the same page or under **API Keys**.

## Automatic recording

Off by default. In the extension's **Options → Automatic recording**, set a
platform to **Auto** and confirm the recording policy once. From then on,
when you open a meeting on that platform:

1. The toolbar icon shows **REC?** (and, if enabled, a notification).
2. One click on the icon, or the keyboard shortcut, starts recording
   immediately: no popup, no picker, no checkbox.
3. Recording stops by itself when you leave the meeting page, close the tab,
   or the meeting goes quiet for a few minutes (3 by default).

That single click is required by Chrome: an extension may only start
capturing after you invoke it. Nothing can start a recording on its own, and
not clicking is how you skip a meeting you do not want recorded.

**Prompt** mode only notifies you and leaves recording to the manual flow.

A self-hosted server can set defaults for everyone with
`RECORDER_AUTO_RECORD_PLATFORMS` (for example `google-meet,zoom,teams` or
`all`), plus `RECORDER_AUTO_STOP_QUIET_SECONDS`, `RECORDER_NOTICE_TEXT` and
`RECORDER_LOCK_DEFAULTS`. Users can still change them in the extension unless
locked. Supported platforms: Google Meet, Zoom (web), Microsoft Teams (web),
Webex, Slack huddles, Whereby, Jitsi Meet. Native desktop apps have no tab to
detect and keep the manual screen-picker flow.

If the keyboard shortcut does nothing, Chrome may have left it unassigned;
the Options page shows the current binding and links to Chrome's shortcut
settings.

## Record a meeting

1. Click the extension icon and choose **Record meeting**.
2. Confirm the participant notice.
3. Talk. Stop from the extension popup, or with the keyboard shortcut
   (`Ctrl+Shift+U` by default).

There is nothing to configure. The recorder uses your system default
microphone, and captures the meeting audio automatically:

- **Meeting in a browser tab** (Google Meet, or Zoom/Teams web): the extension
  records that tab's audio directly. No screen picker, no "share system audio"
  step, and other apps' sounds are not captured.
- **Native Zoom or Teams desktop app**: there is no tab to capture, so the
  extension falls back to the screen picker. Choose **Entire screen** and turn
  on **Share system audio**. On macOS, Chrome asks for the **Screen Recording**
  permission the first time; grant it once.

The microphone is always your system default input. An extension cannot read
which device another tab chose, but the default is almost always the same mic
your meeting app uses. If yours is on a non-default mic, set an override in the
extension's Options.

When you stop, the recording is assembled on the server and appears in your
library shortly after, ready to transcribe and summarize. A local `.webm` copy
is also saved to your Downloads unless you turned that off.

## Reliability

Audio is uploaded in short segments as it is captured and buffered in the
browser's private storage, so a brief network drop or a closed tab loses at
most a few seconds. If the browser closes mid-meeting, the extension offers to
finish uploading what it captured the next time it starts.

## A note on consent

Recording other people can require their consent, and the rules differ by
country and by meeting platform. The notice you confirm before each recording
is a reminder and an accountability record, not consent in itself. You are
responsible for informing participants and complying with the laws and platform
terms that apply to you.
